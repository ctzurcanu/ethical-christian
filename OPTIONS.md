# New Testament Ethical Interpretation Options

## Purpose and Status

This is a proposal bank for New Testament portions that do not presently receive a dedicated ethical analysis in the documentation, or whose existing treatment can be developed substantially. It does not add these declarations to the reconstructed ethos merely by listing them.

Each option distinguishes the strength of the proposed interpretation:

- **explicit**: the relevant ethical relation is stated or enacted directly in the passage;
- **strongly implied**: the declaration generalizes the passage's evident logic;
- **tentative**: the interpretation is plausible but depends on analogy, narrative framing, or a contested reading.

The proposed declarations use the same method as the existing ethos documents: a compact computable name, a witness, a theory mapping, ethical lessons, and an explicit qualification where overextension would corrupt the claim. A practice, office, sacrifice, or vice is not automatically treated as an additional virtue; each is decomposed through the existing virtue axes.

Every Witness link is followed by the complete ISV verse range named by that link. Source section labels and footnote annotations are omitted for readability, while verse numbers are retained. Where a Witness itself names disjoint ranges, `[…]` marks only the interval that the Witness does not cite.

### G1. New Wine Requires a Compatible Institution

**Proposed declaration — `strongly implied`**

> `upgrade_requires_compatible_form`: An ethical upgrade that cannot be inhabited by an existing social form requires a compatible form; otherwise both the upgrade and inherited form may be damaged.

#### Theory Mapping

Inhabitable ethos, ethical upgrade, Governance, Prudence, and Economy. The container is not ethically neutral when its structure determines whether the content survives.

#### Ethical Lessons

- transmission requires a form capable of carrying what is transmitted;
- preserving an inherited procedure is not a higher end than preserving the good it was meant to serve;
- the compatible form may be a rule, office, procedure, or institution; this declaration does not make schism automatically virtuous.

### G4. Representation Can Be Local Rather Than Proximate

**Proposed declaration — `strongly implied`**

> `representation_can_be_local`: A member denied one form of proximity can still be commissioned to represent the ethos where that member's testimony has particular reach.

#### Witness

- [Mark 5:18–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%205%3A18-20&version=ISV)
- [Luke 8:38–39](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%208%3A38-39&version=ISV)

#### Quotes

##### Mark 5:18–20

> **18** As Jesus was getting into the boat, the man who had been demon-possessed kept begging him to let him go with him.
>
> **19** But Jesus wouldn't let him. Instead, he told him, "Go home to your family, and tell them how much the Lord has done for you and how merciful he has been to you."
>
> **20** So the man left and began proclaiming in the Decapolis how much Jesus had done for him. And everyone was utterly amazed.

##### Luke 8:38–39

> **38** Now the man from whom the demons had gone out kept begging Jesus to let him go with him. But Jesus sent him away, saying,
>
> **39** "Go home and tell what God has done for you." So the man left and kept proclaiming throughout the whole city how much Jesus had done for him.

#### Theory Mapping

Representation, Governance, Hope, and Harmony. The restored man is not accepted into the travelling role he requests, but he is given a local representative role.

#### Ethical Lessons

- different placement is not necessarily rejection or lower ethical standing;
- a person's history can make local testimony more competent than distant membership;
- role assignment should follow the good the member can actually produce, not prestige alone.

### G5. Remedy Must Be Verified Until Function Is Restored

**Proposed declaration — `strongly implied`**

> `verify_remedy_until_functional`: A remedy is not complete when an intervention is performed; it is complete when the intended function is observably restored.

#### Witness

- [Mark 8:22–26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%208%3A22-26&version=ISV)

#### Quotes

##### Mark 8:22–26

> **22** As they came to Bethsaida, some people brought a blind man to Jesus and begged him to touch him.
>
> **23** Jesus took the blind man by the hand and led him out of the village. He spit into his eyes, placed his hands on him, and asked him, "Do you see anything?"
>
> **24** The man looked up and said, "I see people, but they look like trees walking around."
>
> **25** Then Jesus placed his hands on the man's eyes again, and he saw clearly. His sight was restored, and he saw everything perfectly even from a distance.
>
> **26** Then Jesus sent him home, saying, "Don't go into the village or tell anyone in the village."

#### Theory Mapping

Measurability, Hope, Economy, Charity, and institutional remedy. Jesus asks what the man can see, receives evidence of partial function, intervenes again, and obtains clear sight.

#### Ethical Lessons

- action performed is not the same measure as outcome achieved;
- the recipient's report is evidence in judging the remedy;
- a partial result should produce another appropriate intervention rather than a false declaration of success.

### G7. Repentance Has Role-Specific Requirements

**Proposed declaration — `explicit`**

> `office_specific_repentance`: Repentance includes correction of the abuses made possible by a person's resources, occupation, and coercive power.

#### Witness

- [Luke 3:10–14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%203%3A10-14&version=ISV)

#### Quotes

##### Luke 3:10–14

> **10** The crowds kept asking him, "What, then, should we do?"
>
> **11** He answered them, "The person who has two coats must share with the one who doesn't have any, and the person who has food must do the same."
>
> **12** Even some tax collectors came to be baptized. They asked him, "Teacher, what should we do?"
>
> **13** He told them, "Stop collecting more money than the amount you are told to collect."
>
> **14** Even some soldiers were asking him, "And what should we do?" He told them, "Never extort money from anyone by threats or blackmail, and be satisfied with your pay."

#### Theory Mapping

Justice, Charity, Market, Governance, Temperance, and Forgiveness. The crowds, tax collectors, and soldiers receive different conduct requirements because they control different goods and risks.

#### Ethical Lessons

- a universal ethos can require role-specific implementations;
- holders of coercive office must renounce extortion, false accusation, and discontent-driven abuse;
- repentance is proven by changed conduct inside the domain where the actor could repeat the wrong.

### G9. Repair Before Removal Has a Deadline

**Proposed declaration — `tentative`**

> `repair_before_removal_has_deadline`: Before removing an unproductive member or institution, a responsible governor may attempt a specific repair for a defined period and then measure again.

2. **Saying 7 — The lion becomes human**

> “Blessed is the lion which the man will eat, so that the lion becomes man; and cursed is the man whom the lion will eat, so that the lion becomes man.”

A strange image of assimilation: whatever consumes you determines what you become.

3. **Saying 13 — Jesus gives Thomas three secret sayings**

Jesus tells Thomas privately three things. When Thomas returns, the others ask what Jesus said. Thomas replies:

> “If I tell you one of the sayings which he said to me, you will take up stones and stone me; and fire will come out of the stones and consume you.”

This dramatic episode is absent from the canonical Gospels.

4. **Saying 18 — The beginning is found where the end is**

> “Have you discovered, then, the beginning, that you look for the end? For where the beginning is, there will the end be.”

History is treated as a circle or a unity rather than a merely linear sequence.

5. **Saying 22 — Becoming like infants means making opposites one**

> “When you make the two one… the male and the female one and the same… then you will enter the kingdom.”

This is much more radical than the canonical saying about receiving the kingdom like a child. It describes salvation as the reunification of opposites.

6. **Saying 42 — Become passers-by**

> “Become passers-by.”

An exceptionally compressed command: do not become attached to the world as a permanent possession.

7. **Saying 56 — The world is a corpse**

> “Whoever has come to understand the world has found merely a corpse, and whoever has found a corpse, of that person the world is not worthy.”

This is one of Thomas’s most starkly anti-worldly statements.

8. **Saying 61 — Two become one**

> “I am not divided. … Where there are two, they are divine; and where there is one, I am with him.”

The saying moves toward a mystical ontology in which division is the fundamental problem.

9. **Saying 70 — Salvation depends on bringing forth what is within**

> “If you bring forth what is within you, what you have will save you. If you do not have that within you, what you do not have within you will kill you.”

This sounds closer to self-realization than to the canonical emphasis on faith, repentance, or obedience.

10. **Saying 77 — Jesus is everywhere**

> “I am the light which is above them all. I am the All. From me did the All come forth, and to me did the All attain. Split a piece of wood, and I am there; lift up the stone, and you will find me there.”

This is perhaps Thomas’s strongest immanent and cosmic saying.

11. **Saying 83 — Images conceal the light**

> “The images are manifest to man, but the light in them remains concealed in the image of the Father’s light.”

Visible reality is treated as an image that hides a deeper divine reality.

12. **Saying 97 — The empty jar**

> “The kingdom of the Father is like a woman carrying a jar full of meal. While she was walking on the road, the handle of the jar broke, and the meal emptied out behind her, without her knowing it. She did not notice the accident. When she reached her house, she put the jar down and found it empty.”

Unlike the usual kingdom parables, this one has no explicit moral or explanation. The kingdom appears as something silently lost—or perhaps unconsciously emptied into the world.

13. **Saying 105 — Knowing one’s parents**

> “Whoever knows the father and the mother will be called the son of a harlot.”

This is deliberately shocking. It may mean that ordinary biological identity is inadequate compared with discovering one’s transcendent origin.

14. **Saying 108 — Becoming what Jesus is**

> “Whoever drinks from my mouth will become as I am; I myself shall become he, and the hidden things shall be revealed to him.”

This goes beyond imitation: the disciple becomes identified with Jesus.

15. **Saying 113 — The kingdom is already spread out**

> “The kingdom of the Father is spread out upon the earth, and people do not see it.”

This is close to Luke 17:21, but Thomas makes the hidden-present character of the kingdom especially explicit.

16. **Saying 114 — Women becoming male**

> “Every woman who makes herself male will enter the kingdom of heaven.”

This is the most controversial saying in the collection. It may reflect ancient symbolic language in which “male” means complete, unified, or spiritually empowered—but in its surviving form it is unmistakably startling and unequal by modern standards.

The most original and philosophically significant group is probably **2, 22, 42, 61, 70, 77, 83, 108, and 113**. They present salvation primarily as awakening, integration, detachment, and recognition of a hidden divine reality—not chiefly as forgiveness of sins or future resurrection.
