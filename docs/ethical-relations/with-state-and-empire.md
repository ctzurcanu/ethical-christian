# With State and Empire

No single New Testament sentence defines the Christian relation to every state. Jesus' coin response distinguishes Caesar's claim from God's without listing everything that belongs to either ([Mark 12:13–17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2012%3A13-17&version=ISV)). Trial and execution narratives expose imperial violence and local collaboration from another angle. Acts alternates compliance, legal appeal, civil protection, refusal, imprisonment, and release. Paul's [Romans 13:1–7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2013%3A1-7&version=ISV) gives governing authority a justice-serving function but leaves abusive rule and competing command under-specified.

The relation must therefore be reconstructed transaction by transaction: taxation, public order, courts, protection, military force, office, worship, speech, punishment, and resistance. The state is an institutional ethos with representatives and proofs of jurisdiction; the church is another. A Christian individual may represent neither in a private act or may hold roles in both.

Later establishment, persecution, toleration, religious liberty, and democratic participation are post-Pauline upgrades, not interpretations already contained in “render to Caesar.”
