# With Other Christian Denominations

Denominations are post-New-Testament institutions. Their relation cannot be reconstructed as if Jesus addressed modern confessional boundaries directly. The relevant evidence begins with each body's public constitution: declarations, ministry, sacraments, membership, discipline, property, and rules for recognizing another church.

The study will distinguish several levels of compatibility: recognizing the other as Christian; recognizing baptism or ministry; sharing communion; transferring members; cooperating in service; issuing joint witness; submitting disputes to a common forum; or composing a new institution. Agreement at one level does not prove agreement at the others.

The Theory of Ethos tests are acknowledgment, alignability, reciprocal declarations, proofs of representation, challengeability, and non-binding membership. Historical condemnations, coercion, current ecumenical agreements, and local practice must be dated and attributed. “Christian unity” is not an ethical result until the members, roles, costs, and decision procedure of the proposed unity are public.
