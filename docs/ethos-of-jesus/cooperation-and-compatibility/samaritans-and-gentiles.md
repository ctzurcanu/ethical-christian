# Samaritans and Gentiles

**Proposed finding — `contested development`**

> `boundary_can_be_crossed`: Israel-priority does not make every Samaritan or Gentile relation hostile; action, request, and response can establish concrete compatibility across the boundary.

The evidence is deliberately uneven. [Matthew 10:5–6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2010%3A5-6&version=ISV) restricts an early mission. The Syrophoenician or Canaanite woman's request in [Mark 7:24–30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%207%3A24-30&version=ISV) and [Matthew 15:21–28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2015%3A21-28&version=ISV) crosses an articulated priority and receives a positive result. Luke makes a Samaritan the model neighbor and depicts another Samaritan as the grateful healed outsider ([Luke 17:11–19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2017%3A11-19&version=ISV)).

These witnesses must not be flattened into a timeless universal policy or used to portray “the Jews” as the incompatible contrast. They show different narrative moments and evangelists' presentations. The post-resurrection extension of membership to peoples beyond Israel becomes a constitutive institutional problem in the ethos of Christ.

**Theory mapping:** partial compatibility, alignability, impartial judgment, geography and audience. **Open challenge:** explain the changing scope without concealing the initial exclusion or the power imbalance in the encounters.
