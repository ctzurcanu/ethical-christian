## Context

Jesus is tired of the coming and going of people and hears the news of John the Baptist being beheaded by Herod. He seeks some privacy to recover.
Elisha inherited the teachings of Elijah. He has proven the transfer of power from his teacher to him by several miracles before this one. One of those miracles was to re-create the splitting of the sea with the river Jordan (a miracle previously performed by Elijah as a proof of ethos inheritance from Moses).
Jesus inherited the teachings of John the Baptist. As per this tradition, it was the moment to prove the inheritance from Moses, Elijah, Elisha.

## Consequential Details

- the people had to be counted, and grouping them facilitated this by means of self-count: "Have them sit down in groups of about 50."
- the leftovers had to be accounted for: “Collect the pieces that are left over so that nothing is wasted.”
- this process of breaking bread and sharing first with disciples, and then disciples with the multitudes is an omen for the spreading of ethos and the creation of Church as institution and hierarchy


## Ethical Lessons

- an ethos can be inherited
- an inheritance can happen contemporaneously or with temporary discontinuity
- an inheritance of ethos has to be proven by the same type of acts
- an ethical inheritance can be upgraded in quantity, quality, or both
- an inheritance proof requires quantitative measurement
- generosity establishes the ethical hierarchy

## Indirect Ethical Lessons

- an ethos can be inherited across long temporal discontinuities without the necessity of reincarnation of its founder/s
