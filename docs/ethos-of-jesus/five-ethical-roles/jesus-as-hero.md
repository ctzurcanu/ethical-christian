# Jesus as Hero

**Role classification — `strongly implied` during life; post-death interpretation belongs to the ethos of Christ**

The Theory of Ethos uses “hero” for one who assumes exceptional responsibility, repairs failures of those formed by the ethos, and influences its moral imagination. Jesus describes his leadership as service and his life as given for others ([Mark 10:42–45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2010%3A42-45&version=ISV)); the good shepherd accepts danger for the flock ([John 10:11–18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2010%3A11-18&version=ISV)). He also enacts the governance he requires, making conduct itself formative.

Those sayings establish voluntary exceptional cost. The claim that his death repairs every follower's wrongdoing is a further interpretation developed by post-death witnesses, not a conclusion to smuggle into this role from later doctrine.

**Ethical function:** assumes more than ordinary member duty and shapes the ethos by exemplary action. **Secondary virtue note:** courage and self-giving help describe the action; the role concerns responsibility and formation.
