# The Mustard Seed

The parable appears in [Matthew 13:31–32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2013%3A31-32&version=ISV), [Mark 4:30–32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%204%3A30-32&version=ISV), and [Luke 13:18–19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2013%3A18-19&version=ISV). Each compares God's kingdom with a very small seed that becomes a large plant in which birds can nest; wording about garden, field, shrub, or tree differs among witnesses.

**Proposed ethos inference — `tentative`**

> `small_beginning_expands_shared_space`: The community governed by God's reign may begin in an unimpressive form and develop into a social space capable of receiving others.

The inference is limited. The stated comparison supports contrast between beginning and developed result; the birds make shelter or inclusion a possible social implication. It does not establish that an ethos has colors, is biologically invasive, grants legal immunity, or “nurtures higher forms of life.” Those properties come from modern descriptions of plants or imaginative extensions rather than the narrated ethical relation.

The seed's being called smallest is ordinary comparative speech in the story, not a universal botanical claim on which the ethos reconstruction depends. Allusions to scriptural tree-and-birds imagery may add kingdom and nations context, but must be argued separately for each Gospel.

This remains in the Source Library because it describes the kingdom's surprising development more directly than it states an interaction rule. It can enter the reconstructed ethos only when linked to clearer evidence about membership, hospitality, mission, or institutional growth.
