# Expected Conduct

Expected conduct is reconstructed from what a community repeatedly teaches, models, funds, rewards, excuses, corrects, and punishes. A list of admired virtues is insufficient. The site asks what one member must do toward another, what leaders owe members, what the institution owes harmed persons, how resources move, how outsiders are treated, and what happens after failure.

Evidence will separate ideals from operating rules. A sermon may declare enemy-love while disciplinary practice rewards retaliation; a charity program may enact care that its doctrinal summary never names; a code may prohibit harm without supplying reporting or repair. Each discrepancy receives its own status.

The initial index will classify conduct by interaction—member/member, member/leader, institution/member, institution/institution, and Christian/outsider—and map it to responsibility, awareness, flexibility, cooperation, proof, and governance. Virtues annotate why one end is preferred only after the interaction is clear.
