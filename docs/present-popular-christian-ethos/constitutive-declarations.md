# Constitutive Declarations

The first task is to identify what a present Christian population treats as non-negotiable, not merely which propositions respondents recognize. Evidence should include membership vows, recurring preaching and liturgy, published statements, decisions made under conflict, budget priorities, and conduct that is praised or sanctioned.

A declaration is constitutive when changing it changes recognized Christian identity or institutional action. “Jesus is Lord,” a creed, biblical authority, salvation language, love of neighbor, a political program, or a worship practice may occupy that place in a specified group, but the site will not assume that all do so everywhere or in the same order.

Each finding must name population, location, institution, period, and evidence type. Declared belief and enacted priority receive separate fields. The resulting question is whether present governing declarations preserve, reorder, or replace those reconstructed for Jesus, the ethos of Christ, Paul, and later institutions.
