# Glossary

**Ethos** — A governing Logos of a person or group whose principal content concerns what ought to be, including declarations, priorities, membership, representation, and sufficient continuity to guide action.

**Ethics / ethical** — The interaction rules between one or more ethoses. In this project, “ethical content” means content that defines or changes those rules or the ethos needed to enact them.

**Ethos of Jesus** — The ethos attributed to Jesus in his teaching and action during his life, reconstructed from distinguishable Gospel witnesses.

**Ethos of Christ** — The shared ethos enacted after Jesus' death by communities that belong to and act in the name of the risen Christ.

**Codification** — The organization of inherited declarations and practices into a portable rule for formation, initiation, recurring life, representation, resources, and governance. The Didache is treated as such a codification.

**Upgrade** — A later addition, specification, reordering, or replacement of an operating ethos rule. The word is descriptive until an ethical audit evaluates the change.

**Declaration** — A compact statement of a constitutive ethos rule, given a stable identifier such as `proof_by_fruits`.

**Protocol** — An ordered interaction specifying actors, steps, evidence, decision, and consequence.

**Representative** — A person or institution authorized to act or speak for another ethos bearer within a defined mandate.

**Enactment** — Observable action through which a declaration becomes operative and testable.

**Compatibility** — The degree to which two ethoses can perform a specified action together without violating their governing declarations. It may be partial and transaction-specific.

**Status** — The evidential classification attached to a reconstruction: `explicit`, `strongly implied`, `tentative`, `contested`, or `contradicted elsewhere`.

**Virtue** — A secondary evaluative quality or ordered end used where it helps explain an interaction; it is not the site's primary unit of organization.
