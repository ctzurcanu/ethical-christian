# Actors and Institutions

Ethical analysis begins by identifying who acts personally, who represents an ethos, and who bears the result. Recurring actors include:

- **Jesus:** teacher and leader during his life; represented Christ after death must be kept analytically distinct.
- **Disciple/member:** an adherent in formation or a participant in a continuing community; not every hearer or beneficiary is a member.
- **The Twelve / apostle / emissary:** roles with different witnesses and scopes; the title alone does not prove a later claim of representation.
- **Prophet and teacher:** itinerant or local contributors tested by message, conduct, effect, and resources.
- **Host / household / family:** counterparties that supply resources, receive representatives, and include dependents with separate interests.
- **Assembly/church:** a corporate bearer capable of initiating, supporting, judging, appointing, sending, and being represented.
- **Bishop, elder, deacon, overseer:** office terms whose relation and stability change across layers.
- **Neighbor, stranger, enemy, outsider:** relation-specific positions, not permanent essences of a person.
- **Jew, Samaritan, Gentile:** historically situated group designations that must not be used as monolithic ethos labels.
- **Temple, synagogue, council, household, city, and empire:** institutions with distinct jurisdiction, representation, and resources.

Every page should state whether an actor is narrator, speaker, addressee, claimant, representative, judge, witness, beneficiary, harmed party, or institution. One actor may occupy several roles, but the transitions must remain visible.
