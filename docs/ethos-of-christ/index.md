# The Ethos of Christ

The ethos of Christ is the communal ethos shared after Jesus' death by people who understand themselves as belonging to, acting in the name of, and representing the risen Christ. It inherits Jesus' ethos but is not identical with it: resurrection proclamation, baptismal membership, the Spirit, apostolic witness, common meals, shared support, local offices, and procedures for admitting Gentiles create new institutional facts.

This layer is reconstructed first from post-death enactment rather than from later doctrinal summaries. Acts supplies narrative evidence; non-Pauline apostolic writings supply distinct witnesses; and the Didache supplies an early, compact codification of formation, ritual, hospitality, testing, and local governance. Paul is held aside until the next layer so that his categories do not silently determine what the earlier shared ethos must have meant.

The order here is analytical: the project reconstructs the Didachean form before applying the Pauline upgrade. It does not by itself settle the disputed dates or literary relationships of the sources.
